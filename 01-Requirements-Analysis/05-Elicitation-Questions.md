# Requirements Elicitation Questions

## Purpose

The following questions would be used by the Business Analyst during stakeholder interviews, workshops and other elicitation activities.

The questions are designed to understand the current student support process, identify problems, establish stakeholder needs and gather information that can be converted into clear business and system requirements.

## Student Questions

1. How do you currently submit a support request?
2. Which communication channel do you normally use?
3. What information do you usually provide when submitting a request?
4. What difficulties do you experience when requesting support?
5. How do you currently know whether your request has been received?
6. How do you know who is handling your request?
7. How do you currently receive updates about your request?
8. How long do you normally wait for a response?
9. What would make the support process easier for you?
10. What information would you expect to see when checking the status of your request?

## Support Staff Questions

1. How are student support requests currently received?
2. How are requests recorded after they are received?
3. What information is captured for each request?
4. How are requests assigned to staff members?
5. How do you determine which requests should be handled first?
6. What happens when a request is received through more than one communication channel?
7. How do you identify requests that have not yet been resolved?
8. How do you know when a request becomes overdue?
9. What parts of the current process require the most manual work?
10. What information would make it easier for you to manage requests?
11. What problems occur when important information is missing from a request?
12. What would an improved support process need to provide?

## Support Manager Questions

1. How do you currently monitor the Student Support Department?
2. What information do you need to understand the department's workload?
3. How do you identify overdue or unresolved requests?
4. What reports do you currently prepare?
5. How much time is spent manually preparing reports?
6. What performance measures are important to you?
7. What information would help you identify areas where the department is struggling?
8. How should requests be prioritised?
9. What information should management be able to view?
10. What would indicate that the improved process is successful?

## IT / System Administrator Questions

1. What systems or tools are currently used to manage support requests?
2. What technical limitations exist with the current tools?
3. What user roles would need access to the system?
4. What information should different users be allowed to view or change?
5. What security requirements should be considered?
6. What information needs to be protected?
7. Are there existing systems that the improved solution would need to integrate with?
8. What technical constraints could affect the proposed solution?
9. What maintenance or support would the system require?
10. What availability and performance expectations should be considered?

## Academic / Faculty Staff Questions

1. Which types of student requests are normally referred to academic or faculty staff?
2. What information do you need to resolve an academic-related request?
3. How are requests currently referred to you?
4. What problems occur when requests are referred between departments?
5. How should referred requests be tracked?
6. What information would help you respond to requests more efficiently?

## Cross-Stakeholder Questions

The following questions can be used with multiple stakeholder groups to identify common needs and conflicting expectations:

1. What is the biggest problem with the current process?
2. What part of the current process works well?
3. What should be improved first?
4. What information is essential for managing a support request?
5. What should happen when a request cannot be resolved immediately?
6. What should happen when a request is assigned to the wrong person?
7. What information should be visible to different users?
8. What would make the process more efficient?
9. What risks or concerns would you have about changing the current process?
10. How would you measure whether the new process has improved the situation?

## Follow-Up Questions

The Business Analyst should use follow-up questions when an answer is unclear or incomplete.

Examples include:

- Can you explain what you mean by that?
- Can you give me an example?
- What happens after that?
- Why does that cause a problem?
- Who is responsible for that step?
- What information is required at that point?
- What would you expect to happen instead?
- How often does this occur?
- What would happen if this requirement was not met?

## Elicitation Principle

The Business Analyst should listen carefully to stakeholder responses, ask clarifying questions and avoid assuming that the first answer represents the complete requirement.

Information gathered from different stakeholders should be compared and validated before being documented as a confirmed requirement.
