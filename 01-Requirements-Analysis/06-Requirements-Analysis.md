# Requirements Analysis

## Purpose

Requirements analysis is the process of reviewing the information gathered during elicitation to determine what the business and stakeholders actually need from the proposed improvement.

The requirements must be clear, relevant, complete, realistic, consistent and testable.

For this fictional case study, the findings below represent simulated stakeholder findings based on the business problem and elicitation plan.

## Simulated Elicitation Findings

The following findings were identified from the simulated stakeholder perspectives:

### Students

- Students need a simple way to submit support requests.
- Students need confirmation that their request has been received.
- Students want to know the current status of their request.
- Students need clear communication when additional information is required.
- Students want reasonable response times.

### Support Staff

- Staff need all support requests to be recorded consistently.
- Staff need access to requests assigned to them.
- Staff need to update the status of requests.
- Staff need to identify urgent and overdue requests.
- Staff need sufficient information to resolve requests.
- Staff need to avoid duplicate records and unnecessary manual data entry.

### Support Manager

- Managers need visibility of all support requests.
- Managers need to monitor outstanding and overdue requests.
- Managers need to view workload across support staff.
- Managers need reliable information for reporting.
- Managers need to identify trends and areas requiring improvement.

### IT / System Administrator

- The solution needs controlled access based on user roles.
- Sensitive student information needs to be protected.
- The system needs to be reliable and maintainable.
- User access and permissions need to be manageable.

## Requirement Analysis Criteria

Each potential requirement should be reviewed against the following criteria:

| Criterion | Analysis Question |
|---|---|
| Clear | Is the requirement easy to understand? |
| Complete | Does it contain enough information? |
| Relevant | Does it address a genuine business or stakeholder need? |
| Consistent | Does it conflict with another requirement? |
| Feasible | Can the requirement realistically be implemented? |
| Testable | Can the requirement be verified or tested? |
| Traceable | Can the requirement be linked back to a business problem or stakeholder need? |
| Prioritised | Is its importance to the business understood? |

## Requirement Issues Identified

The analysis identified several areas that require particular attention:

### Multiple Communication Channels

Requests currently arrive through email, WhatsApp, telephone calls and in-person visits. This creates a risk that information will remain separated across different channels.

**Analysis:** A consistent method of recording requests is required.

### Duplicate Requests

A student may contact the department through more than one channel about the same issue.

**Analysis:** The process should support identification and management of potential duplicate requests.

### Unclear Responsibility

Staff may not always know who is responsible for handling a request.

**Analysis:** Requests should have a clear owner or assigned staff member.

### Limited Status Visibility

Students and managers have limited visibility of the progress of requests.

**Analysis:** Request status should be recorded and updated throughout the support process.

### Overdue Requests

The current process makes it difficult to identify requests that have not been handled within the expected timeframe.

**Analysis:** The process should support monitoring of outstanding and overdue requests.

### Manual Reporting

Managers currently spend time manually compiling information for reporting.

**Analysis:** Relevant support information should be structured so that accurate reports can be produced more efficiently.

## Requirement Dependencies

Some requirements depend on other requirements being implemented first.

For example:

- A request must be recorded before it can be assigned.
- A request must be assigned before responsibility can be tracked.
- A request must have a status before its progress can be monitored.
- Accurate request information must be recorded before meaningful reports can be produced.
- User roles and permissions must be defined before access can be controlled.

Understanding these dependencies will help with requirement prioritisation and later solution planning.

## Requirement Conflicts and Considerations

Different stakeholders may have competing needs.

For example, students may want to provide as little information as possible when submitting a request, while support staff may require detailed information to resolve the issue efficiently.

Similarly, management may require detailed reporting information, while privacy and security considerations may restrict which student information should be included in reports.

These differences should be discussed and resolved during requirements validation.

## Requirement Traceability

Each requirement should eventually be linked to:

- The original business problem.
- The relevant stakeholder need.
- The business objective it supports.
- The expected business outcome.
- Appropriate acceptance criteria.

This will help demonstrate that the proposed requirements are connected to the original reason for improving the student support process.

## Analysis Conclusion

The analysis indicates that CLI requires a more structured and consistent approach to managing student support requests.

The most important areas identified are request capture, assignment, status tracking, prioritisation, overdue monitoring, information quality, access control and management reporting.

These findings will be used to develop the detailed business, functional and non-functional requirements in the next section.
